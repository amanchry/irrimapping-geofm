    Foundation models in this study¶

    This page describes how the six foundation models are adapted to this study: the bands and months they receive, how a 128 × 128 px tile is cut into tokens, what is merged where, and which parts are frozen or trained. The original design of each model, as its authors built and pretrained it, is on the model pages (overview, Prithvi-EO-2.0, Clay v1.5, TerraMind, AgriFM, Presto, TESSERA). How they are trained and scored is on the experiment pages (E04, E05).

    Configuration in this study¶

    Every model classifies every 10 m pixel (D16). The encoders see whole 128 × 128 px tiles (1.28 km) of 12 monthly Sentinel-2 composites of the agricultural year, with the bands, band order, normalisation and weights of their own pretraining. Every input uses Sentinel-2 only (D14): every model sees the same optical data, and Sentinel-1 coverage of Utah changed between the years. All six encoders are loaded by pixelwise_run/encoders.py.

    How much of the encoder is trained differs between the experiments (RQ2 · Fine-tuning explains the strategies):

    Experiment Models Encoder Trained
    E04 (RQ1) all six frozen: pretrained weights, no gradient updates, evaluation mode head only (1.7–3.2 M weights, depending on the encoder)
    E05 (RQ2) Prithvi-EO-2.0, Morocco first frozen, LoRA (low-rank adapters, rank 16, on the attention and MLP layers), fully fine-tuned (every weight, encoder learning rate 10⁻⁵, head 10⁻⁴) or from scratch (same network from random weights); 10, 25, 50 or 100% of the training parcels head only (frozen), 5.5 M adapters + head (LoRA), or all ≈ 306 M weights

    What this means for the comparison¶

    • Spatial unit. The image ViTs (Prithvi, TerraMind 160 m; Clay 80 m) decide on a coarse grid that the head upsamples to 10 m, so field edges are soft; AgriFM starts at 40 m; Presto and TESSERA decide per 10 m pixel but see no neighbours, which the UPerNet head adds afterwards.
    • Time. Prithvi, Clay and TerraMind encode months separately; the L-TAE combines them. AgriFM, Presto and TESSERA combine the months inside the encoder.
    • Pretraining gap. Prithvi was pretrained on 30 m HLS, AgriFM partly on 30 m and 250 m data, the others at 10 m; Presto, TESSERA and Prithvi saw time series, Clay and TerraMind single dates.
    • Inputs left out. TerraMind, Presto and TESSERA were pretrained with Sentinel-1 (and Presto with weather and terrain); here they receive Sentinel-2 only.

    Settings used in this study (details)¶

    One subsection per model: what is taken from the original design and what is changed for the tiles of this study. The original design is on the model's own page.

    Prithvi-EO-2.0¶

    Prithvi-EO-2.0 is pretrained on HLS time series and encodes acquisition date and location explicitly. The six HLS bands (Blue, Green, Red, Narrow NIR, SWIR 1, SWIR 2; Sentinel-2 B2, B3, B4, B8A, B11, B12) are standardised with the model's pretraining statistics. Following the pretraining configuration, four frames are processed per pass (September–December, January–April, May–August); each frame retains its own tokens, giving one vector per month. The model is loaded through TerraTorch (prithvi_eo_v2_300_tl). Limitation: pretraining used 30 m HLS data, whereas 10 m Sentinel-2 is used here; the resulting 160 m patch is closer to typical parcel size than the 480 m patch at native resolution.

    Original design: Prithvi-EO-2.0.

    From one tile to a class per 10 m pixel (Prithvi-EO-2.0)¶

    One 128 × 128 px tile through Prithvi-EO-2.0 and the trained head, with the shape of the data after every step (one tile; grey = frozen encoder, orange = trained head). Schematic: the maps are illustrations, the sizes are those of the model (pixelwise_run/encoders.py, pixelwise_run/tile_common.py). Drawn by tools/a11_geofm_pipeline_figures.py.

    Step What happens Output for one tile Trained in E04
    0 · Input 12 monthly composites, 6 bands, standardised with Prithvi's statistics; pixels without a clear observation set to 0 and masked 12 × 6 × 128 × 128 –
    1 · Encoder 3 passes of 4 months; each month cut into 8 × 8 patches of 16 × 16 px; a patch (16 × 16 px × 6 bands = 1,536 numbers) is projected to a token of 1,024 values, date and location embeddings are added, and 24 transformer layers let every token attend to the 256 tokens of its pass 12 × 1,024 × 8 × 8 no (304 M weights)
    2 · L-TAE at each of the 64 patch positions the 12 monthly vectors are reduced to 256 values and weighted by 16 attention heads (months without a clear pixel in the patch masked) 256 × 8 × 8 yes (0.36 M)
    3 · Feature pyramid four map sizes from the 8 × 8 map: × 4 and × 2 by transposed convolutions, × 1 by a 1 × 1 convolution, × ½ by max-pooling 128 × 32², 16², 8², 4² yes (0.39 M)
    4 · UPerNet pyramid pooling on the 4 × 4 map, top-down sums from coarse to fine, fusion of the four sizes at 32 × 32, 1 × 1 convolution to the two classes 2 × 32 × 32 (40 m cells) yes (1.72 M)
    5 · Upsampling bilinear interpolation × 4; the loss is the class-weighted cross-entropy at the labelled pixels only 2 × 128 × 128 (10 m) –

    Width of a token. The 1,024 values per token are the width of the encoder (ViT-Large), a property of the model, not of the input: every patch, whatever its content, becomes 1,024 values, and only the values differ. The input sets the number of tokens (8 × 8 per month for a 128 px tile with 16 px patches), not their length.

    One vector per 160 m patch, not per pixel. The encoder describes the 256 pixels of a 16 × 16 px patch with one vector per month; no per-pixel embedding exists inside Prithvi. The 10 m classes are produced by the head: the pyramid and UPerNet decide on 40 m cells using the neighbouring patches and the coarser context, and the final bilinear interpolation spreads those decisions to 10 m. Field edges inside a patch are therefore resolved only as far as the head can infer them from the patch and its neighbours.

    Why a pyramid. A plain vision transformer keeps one resolution throughout (8 × 8 here), whereas segmentation heads combine fine maps, which locate boundaries, with coarse maps, which carry context. The simple feature pyramid of ViTDet (Li et al., 2022), used for plain ViTs in PANGAEA (Marsocci et al., 2024), derives four sizes from the last layer alone. The upsampled maps are learnt from the 8 × 8 map and add no new spatial information.

    How UPerNet merges the sizes (Xiao et al., 2018). (1) Pyramid pooling: the 4 × 4 map is averaged to 1 × 1, 2 × 2 and 4 × 4 cells, brought back to 4 × 4 and concatenated, which gives every cell the context of the whole tile; pool sizes 1, 2, 4 replace the usual 1, 2, 3, 6 because the coarsest map has only 4 × 4 cells. (2) Top-down path: each coarser map is upsampled and added to the next finer one (4 → 8 → 16 → 32), so that the fine maps also carry context. (3) Fusion: the four maps are upsampled to 32 × 32, concatenated and mixed by a 3 × 3 convolution; a 1 × 1 convolution then gives two class scores per 40 m cell.

    L-TAE (Garnot & Landrieu, 2020). Each head has one learned query that scores the 12 months of a patch; the softmax of the scores weights the months, and the weighted sums of the 16 heads form the 256-value output. The weights are learned from the labels, so months that separate irrigated from rainfed fields (for example the dry summer, when only irrigated fields stay green) can receive more weight.

    Clay v1.5¶

    Clay encodes each band together with its central wavelength, which allows a single model to process imagery from different sensors. Ten Sentinel-2 bands (B2–B8A, B11, B12) are standardised with the statistics of Clay's metadata.yaml. Additional inputs are the pixel size (10 m), the acquisition date (week of year, sine/cosine encoded; monthly composites are dated mid-month) and the location (latitude and longitude, sine/cosine encoded). The model processes one date per forward pass, so the twelve months are embedded independently. With 8 px patches, a 128 px tile yields a 16 × 16 token grid (8 × 8 for the 64 px chips of the archived parcel benchmark).

    Original design: Clay v1.5.

    What differs from Prithvi. Clay's 8 px patches give a 16 × 16 token grid (80 m), so the head decides on 20 m cells, the finest of the three image ViTs. The patch projection is not a fixed matrix: its weights are generated from each band's central wavelength, which lets one model read different sensors. Each month is a separate pass, so attention never links months inside the encoder; the L-TAE alone combines them. The feature pyramid, UPerNet and L-TAE steps are explained in detail under Prithvi-EO-2.0.

    TerraMind-1.0-base¶

    TerraMind is trained to generate any of nine modalities from any other and therefore learns a joint representation of optical, radar and auxiliary data. The pixel-wise experiments supply only S2L2A (B1–B12), standardised with TerraMind's pretraining statistics (D14). The Sentinel-1 ablation of the archived parcel benchmark also supplied S1GRD (VV, VH in dB) and averaged the tokens of the two modalities (merge_method = "mean"). One date is processed per pass; 16 px patches give an 8 × 8 token grid on a 128 px tile (4 × 4 on a 64 px chip).

    Original design: TerraMind-1.0-base.

Only one of TerraMind's nine modalities is given (Sentinel-2 L2A). The feature pyramid, UPerNet and L-TAE steps are explained in detail under Prithvi-EO-2.0.

    AgriFM¶

    AgriFM is designed for crop mapping and models the temporal dimension within the network. The full 12-month stack of ten Sentinel-2 bands is processed in a single pass. Input tokens span four time steps and 4 × 4 px (the pretrained patch embedding); the final feature map has a stride of 32 px (D12). Because time is compressed inside the network, there is one set of features per season: in the pixel-wise experiments its four-level feature pyramid goes directly to the UPerNet head; the archived parcel benchmark pooled it to one vector per parcel. Only the encoder is used, through the mmengine registries of the cloned repository.

    Normalisation (D15). Neither the AgriFM paper nor the repository documents the normalisation of the pretraining data; the only published statistics belong to the repository's fine-tuning example (configs/cropland_mapping.py, blue 4,179 ± 4,042). The cloud-masked composites of this study are on a different scale (Morocco blue 714 ± 344), so with the config statistics the visible and NIR bands would use only 8–30% of the expected spread. AgriFM is therefore normalised per band with the mean and standard deviation of the region's own chips (NORM = "data"; statistics saved to agrifm_norm_stats_<tag>.json), the usual choice when the pretraining statistics are unknown. The config statistics are kept as a sensitivity run (NORM = "config", file agrifm-cfgnorm_*.npz).

    Original design: AgriFM.

    Step What happens Output for one tile Trained in E04
    0 · Input 12 monthly composites, 10 bands, standardised with the region's band statistics (D15); pixels without a clear observation set to 0 12 × 10 × 128 × 128 –
    1 · 3-D patches the 12-month stack is cut into blocks of 4 months × 4 × 4 px (640 numbers), each projected to 128 values 128 × 3 × 32 × 32 (3,072 tokens) no

    4 · Upsampling bilinear × 4; loss at the labelled pixels only 2 × 128 × 128 (10 m) –

    What differs from the image ViTs. AgriFM reads the whole year in one pass, as a 12-frame video, and reduces time inside the network, so it needs neither an L-TAE nor an added pyramid: its hierarchical Swin stages already produce four map sizes, which go straight to UPerNet (whose larger input widths explain its 3.23 M weights). The finest map (32 × 32, 40 m) is the patch embedding itself, before any attention, so the finest level carries little context; the coarser maps come from 2, 4 and 22 Swin blocks. The fourth stage (2 blocks at 4 × 4) runs but its output is not used. The UPerNet steps are explained under Prithvi-EO-2.0.

    Presto¶

    Presto tokenises a pixel's time series by channel group and month (Sentinel-1, three Sentinel-2 groups, NIR, SWIR, ERA5, SRTM, NDVI, Dynamic World) and adds the month and the location. Sentinel-2 only (D14): the Sentinel-1, ERA5, SRTM and Dynamic World tokens are masked, which the model supports (masked tokens are removed). Sentinel-2 is divided by 10,000 as in pretraining. Presto requires the same tokens to be masked for all pixels of a batch, so months without a clear observation are filled by linear interpolation in time. The embedding is the normalised mean of the encoder tokens (128-d).

    Original design: Presto.

    1 · Encoder, once per pixel each pixel's series is cut into 6 band groups × 12 months = 72 tokens plus a location token; 2 transformer layers (width 128); the mean of the tokens is the pixel's embedding; 16,384 runs per tile 128 × 128 × 128 no (0.4 M)

    What differs from the image models. Presto never sees a neighbouring pixel: one run encodes one pixel, and its tokens are that pixel's band groups and months, not image patches. The encoder therefore delivers a true 10 m map, and the pyramid is plain average pooling, which gives each pixel the context of its 20, 40 and 80 m neighbourhood; spatial patterns enter only through the UPerNet head. 16,384 runs per tile is cheap because the encoder is small (0.4 M weights). The UPerNet steps are explained under Prithvi-EO-2.0.

    TESSERA v2¶

    TESSERA encodes the Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1 observations of a pixel as unordered sets dated by their day of year and pools them with attention into a 128-d embedding (Matryoshka-ordered: the first 16, 32 or 64 dimensions are usable on their own). The Medium student is used, as recommended by the authors. TESSERA was trained on single acquisitions; here each observation is a monthly composite dated the 15th of its month, and only clear months are used, padded or subsampled to the observation-count bin of the reference code (8 or 16). Sentinel-2 only: the Sentinel-1 branch receives one empty observation, as the reference code does without Sentinel-1.

    Original design: TESSERA v2.

Padding repeats some clear months (for 10 clear months, six are repeated to reach 16 tokens, following the reference inference code). The UPerNet steps are explained under Prithvi-EO-2.0.

    Earlier parcel-wise benchmark (archived)¶

    The first benchmark (archive) classified whole parcels in Morocco with the four image models, all frozen. Each model received a 64 × 64 px chip (640 m) centred on the parcel, and the parcel embedding was the mean of the patch tokens covering the parcel, weighted by the fraction of each patch inside the parcel (Models pipeline §5). These embeddings cannot give wall-to-wall maps, which is why the study moved to the pixel-wise set-up (D16).

    An optional ablation of that benchmark (Morocco only) added Sentinel-1 where a model was pretrained on it: TerraMind with Sentinel-1 (MODALITIES = ["S2L2A", "S1GRD"] in script 02c, written as terramind-s1s2_agyear_tiles.npz) and the baseline with radar features (S1S2_VARIABLES = "all" in script 03).
