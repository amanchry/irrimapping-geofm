    Foundation models: overview¶

    Six geospatial foundation models (GeoFMs) are compared in this study. This section describes each model as its authors designed and pretrained it, from the paper, model card and code. How the models are adapted to the tiles, bands and labels of this study is a separate page: Foundation models in this study.

    What a geospatial foundation model is¶

    A foundation model is a large network trained once, without task labels or with cheap labels, on a very large and diverse set of satellite data, so that its internal representation (the embedding) is useful for many later tasks. A task such as irrigated / rainfed classification then needs only a small model on top of the embedding (a head), trained on few labels, or a light adaptation of the network (fine-tuning).

    flowchart LR A["Large unlabelled archive<br/>(Sentinel-2, Landsat, Sentinel-1, …)"] --> B["Pretraining task<br/>e.g. rebuild hidden patches"] B --> C["Pretrained encoder<br/>(weights published)"] C --> D["Embedding of a new image<br/>or pixel time series"] D --> E["Small task head<br/>trained on few labels"] E --> F["Task output<br/>e.g. irrigated / rainfed"]

    Why these six¶

    The models were selected because (i) their pretrained weights are openly available and can be fine-tuned, (ii) they accept multi-temporal Sentinel-2 (and in part Sentinel-1) input, and (iii) together they cover the main GeoFM families:

    Family Models Idea
    Masked-autoencoder Vision Transformers Prithvi-EO-2.0, Clay v1.5 cut images into patches, hide most of them, learn to rebuild them
    Multimodal generative transformer TerraMind learn to translate between many data types (optical, radar, elevation, land cover, text)
    Agriculture-specific spatio-temporal model AgriFM a video transformer over image time series, pretrained to predict land-cover fractions
    Pixel time-series models Presto, TESSERA encode the time series of a single pixel, not image patches

    How they differ in principle¶

    • Image vs pixel. Prithvi, Clay, TerraMind and AgriFM look at image patches and see the neighbourhood of a location; Presto and TESSERA look at one pixel at a time and see only its spectral time series.
    • Time. Prithvi was pretrained on short image time series, AgriFM on longer ones, Presto and TESSERA on full years of a pixel; Clay and TerraMind on single dates, so they have no built-in notion of a season.
    • Sensors. Clay accepts any sensor through wavelength-aware band embeddings; TerraMind combines nine modalities; Prithvi is tied to the six HLS bands; Presto and TESSERA combine Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 (Presto also weather, terrain and land cover).
    • Objective. Four models learn by reconstructing or predicting hidden parts of the input, TESSERA by making two views of the same pixel agree, and AgriFM by supervised prediction of land-cover fractions.
    • Size. From 0.4 M weights (Presto) to about 300 M (Prithvi, Clay); TESSERA distils a 2 B teacher into small students.
