    E05 · Prithvi: frozen, LoRA, fine-tuned or from scratch (RQ2)¶

    Status: Morocco done, all 16 runs (22 November 2026); Utah later

    Pretraining matters, training the encoder does not: frozen, LoRA and full fine-tuning all reach macro-F1 0.90–0.92 at every share of the labels, about 0.09 above the same network trained from scratch (0.80–0.83; significant at every share, except frozen at 50%). Frozen vs LoRA vs full fine-tuning: no difference counts. Results

    Set-up

    Code: experiments/E05_prithvi_strategies/ and pixelwise_run/tile_common.py, ready and tested. Same labelled pixels as E01–E04: Morocco 2023/24 and 2024/25 pooled, 473,675 pixels in 2,015 tiles (7,579 irrigated and 1,196 rainfed parcels), two classes. 16 GPU jobs: 4 strategies × 4 label shares.

    Questions.

    1. How do the four ways of training the same network behave: speed of learning, overfitting, stability?
    2. What does training the encoder (a few adapter weights or every weight) cost (trained weights, GPU time) and what does it change compared with the frozen encoder of E04?
    3. Does full fine-tuning beat the frozen encoder and the same network trained from scratch, does parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) keep the gain at a fraction of the trained weights, and do the answers depend on the number of labelled parcels?

    What changes from E04. Only the encoder: whether it starts from the pretrained weights and whether its weights are trained (none, low-rank adapters, or all). Network, tiles, labelled pixels, folds, loss and metrics are those of E04, so the four strategies differ in nothing else. A fourth factor, the share of training parcels (10, 25, 50, 100%), shows where pretraining pays off. Prithvi is the only encoder: it is the model of the study that motivated E05 (below), a plain ViT whose architecture TerraTorch builds with random weights as easily as with pretrained ones, and the GeoFM for which LoRA has been compared with full fine-tuning (Marti-Escofet et al. 2025). One encoder keeps the cost at 16 jobs.

    The question follows a recent study that fine-tuned Prithvi-EO-2.0 for crop-type mapping of smallholder farms (Tomotaki-Dawoud et al. 2025): full fine-tuning gave a macro-F1 of 0.79 against 0.56 for the frozen encoder and 0.63 from scratch. That study used one validation split, no confidence intervals, three dates of 30 m HLS imagery resampled to 0.5 m, and 601 parcels. E05 repeats the comparison with spatial cross-validation, block-bootstrap confidence intervals and paired tests, 12 monthly composites of 10 m Sentinel-2, and a label-share curve. LoRA is added because, for Prithvi-EO-2.0 on five segmentation tasks, it matched full fine-tuning with 1.8% of the encoder's weights trained and generalised better to held-out regions (Marti-Escofet et al. 2025).

    How E05 works, step by step¶

    E05 trains the same Prithvi network in four ways, and each strategy treats the encoder differently (locked, locked with adapters, fully trained from the pretrained weights, or fully trained from random weights). Each strategy therefore has its own figure, from the labelled pixels to the paired tests it enters (schematic: the pictures illustrate the steps and show no results, so the figures hold for any region and year; tools/a9_experiment_explainers.py). Grey parts are locked, orange parts are trained. The results of each region are under Results.

    The pretrained encoder stays locked, as in E04; only the L-TAE and the head learn (2.5 M weights). The reference the other strategies are measured against.

    The pretrained encoder stays locked, but thin low-rank adapters (rank 16) next to its attention and MLP weights are trained with the head (8.0 M weights); gradients pass through the whole encoder.

    Every weight is trained, starting from the pretrained ones, with the encoder at a ten times smaller learning rate than the head (306 M weights).

    The same network starts from random weights and learns everything from the labelled pixels at the head's learning rate (306 M weights): what the network achieves without pretraining.

    The twelve steps, the same for every strategy except step 6:

    1. Labelled pixels and tiles. As in E03 and E04: whole 128 px tiles, scored at the same labelled pixels as every experiment.
    2. The strategy. One of four ways to train the same network; the figure highlights it among the four.
    3. Label shares. Each strategy is run with 10, 25, 50 and 100% of the training parcels (one job each); parcels are dropped per class in a fixed nested order, and the validation and test folds stay complete.
    4. Rotation over the folds. The same five rounds as every experiment.
    5. Input and tokens. As E04 Prithvi: six bands, three passes of four months with dates and location, 16 px patches.
    6. The encoder. Locked (frozen), locked with trained adapters (LoRA), fully trained from the pretrained weights at a tenfold smaller learning rate (full fine-tuning), or fully trained from random weights (from scratch).
    7. Head. The L-TAE, feature pyramid and UPerNet head of E04, trained in every strategy.
    8. Training. Early stopping on the validation pixels (patience 15, at most 60 epochs), one epoch of warm-up, gradient clipping, three seeds per round.
    9. What is kept and its cost. Trained weights, learning rates, the parts saved at the best epoch, and whether the backward pass runs through the encoder; GPU time per run in summary.csv.
    10. Learning curves. Training loss, validation loss and validation macro-F1 per epoch.
    11. Score per share. Each run scored on all out-of-fold pixels with its 95% interval, against its label share.
    12. Paired tests. At each share, the pairs the strategy enters (e.g. fine-tuned − frozen, LoRA − frozen, fine-tuned − scratch), on the same pixels and bootstrap draws, Holm-corrected over the 24 comparisons.

    Why the pyramid is built from one layer

    A plain ViT returns one map at one resolution (one vector per 16 px patch), but UPerNet combines several resolutions. PANGAEA (Feature2Pyramid, with the scales 4, 2, 1, ½) and the MMSegmentation configurations for BEiT and MAE rescale four different encoder layers (for a 12-block ViT, blocks 4, 6, 8 and 12). ViTDet showed that a pyramid rescaled from the last layer alone is sufficient (Li et al. 2022); E04 and E05 use this simpler form, which needs one L-TAE instead of four. All four maps have the same true resolution (16 px): the ×4 and ×2 maps are learnt upsamplings, so the decision grid is 40 m and field edges are soft. The head is identical in the four strategies, so this choice does not favour any of them. How UPerNet works: E04.

    Frozen

    Frozen is E04 for Prithvi. The encoder describes each patch as it learnt in pretraining; the head learns to read these descriptions. It cannot add a distinction the pretrained features do not contain.

    Full fine-tuning

    Full fine-tuning starts from the same weights but lets the error of the labels flow back into all 24 blocks, so the features themselves move towards irrigated / rainfed. The encoder gets a ten times smaller learning rate than the new head: the head starts from random weights and must learn fast, the encoder should only shift. Different learning rates for different parts of a pretrained network are the "discriminative fine-tuning" of Howard & Ruder (2018), here in its simplest two-group form. A large step on a pretrained encoder can distort the pretrained features and generalise worse to data unlike the training data (Kumar et al. 2022), which matters for the year-transfer test (RQ3).

    LoRA (low-rank adaptation)

    How LoRA works (a trainable low-rank update B·A next to each frozen weight matrix, B starting at zero) is explained with a diagram on RQ2 · Fine-tuning. In E05: rank 16, α = 32, on the qkv attention layer and the two MLP layers (fc1, fc2) of all 24 Prithvi blocks, following Marti-Escofet et al. (2025); about 5.5 M trainable weights, 1.8% of the encoder. Memory stays close to the frozen run: no gradients or optimiser states for the 304 M encoder weights.

    From scratch

    The same 300 M network with random weights, trained like the fine-tuned one (encoder and head at 10⁻⁴). The difference to full fine-tuning is the starting point only, so it isolates the value of pretraining. A ViT-Large has far more weights than a few thousand parcels can determine; it is expected to be the weakest at small label shares. It is a reference for this network, not the best model trained from scratch: that is U-TAE (E03, 1.15 M weights).

    Fewer labels¶

    Training¶

    Identical for the four strategies except the learning rates above:

    Item Choice
    Labels labelled pixels of the round's training folds (of the kept parcels); all other pixels ignored in the loss
    Loss class-weighted cross-entropy (weights from the kept training pixels)
    Optimiser AdamW, weight decay 10⁻⁴, batch 8 tiles, bfloat16 mixed precision
    Warm-up learning rate rises linearly over the first epoch, then constant (Goyal et al. 2017); avoids large first steps on the randomly initialised head and encoder
    Gradient clipping total gradient norm ≤ 1
    Augmentation random flips and 90° rotations
    Early stopping macro-F1 of the validation pixels; patience 15, at most 60 epochs (E04: 8 and 50; longer here so that the curves show the overfitting phase)
    Seeds 3 per round (42, 1042, 2042), mean probability; the seed also sets the initial weights of the head, of the LoRA adapters and, from scratch, of the encoder
    Logged per epoch training loss, validation loss (the same weighted cross-entropy), validation macro-F1 and rainfed F1, seconds

    The frozen run at 100% repeats E04 Prithvi with a longer patience, warm-up and gradient clipping; its score should lie close to E04's and is a check of the code.

    Settings not tuned: the learning rates (10⁻⁴ is E04's; 10⁻⁵ for the encoder is the tenfold reduction; the LoRA adapters use the head's 10⁻⁴, since they start at zero and are small), the LoRA rank, weight decay and the head. They are the same for every share.

    How to run¶

    Script: experiments/E05_prithvi_strategies/run.py (settings at the top; strategy and share from EXPERIMENT_STRATEGY and EXPERIMENT_FRACTION). One GPU job per strategy and share; needs the Morocco tile store outputs/morocco/data_training_prep/03_tiles_128px/agyear/tiles/, the pixel samples outputs/morocco/data_training_prep/04_pixel_samples/ and the Prithvi weights in checkpoints/prithvi/.

    Quick test first, one per strategy (60 tiles, 2 epochs, 1 seed; too short to learn the rainfed class, it reads the epoch time and GPU memory):

    for s in frozen lora finetune scratch; do sbatch --export=ALL,SCRIPT=experiments/E05_prithvi_strategies/run.py,EXPERIMENT_REGION=morocco,EXPERIMENT_STRATEGY=$s,EXPERIMENT_FRACTION=0.5,EXPERIMENT_QUICK_TEST=1 jobs/step_gpu.sbatch; done

    Then the 16 runs:

    for s in frozen lora finetune scratch; do for f in 0.1 0.25 0.5 1.0; do sbatch --time=72:00:00 --export=ALL,SCRIPT=experiments/E05_prithvi_strategies/run.py,EXPERIMENT_REGION=morocco,EXPERIMENT_STRATEGY=$s,EXPERIMENT_FRACTION=$f jobs/step_gpu.sbatch; done; done

    After copying results/ to the Mac (report.py uses whatever runs are finished):

    python experiments/E05_prithvi_strategies/report.py

    Compute (estimate, to be replaced by the quick-test times). Frozen Prithvi took about 75 s per epoch on the Morocco tiles in the earlier runs. Fine-tuning and training from scratch also run the backward pass through the 300 M encoder: in local tests about 1.8 (fine-tuning) and 2.4 (from scratch) times the frozen time per epoch; LoRA about 1.3 times. With the longer patience, roughly 140 GPU hours for the 16 jobs.

    Outputs¶

    experiments/E05_prithvi_strategies/results/morocco/<strategy>_p<share>/ (e.g. finetune_p025/, lora_p100/): the E01 files plus training_history.csv (per epoch: training loss, validation loss, validation macro-F1 and rainfed F1, seconds) and label_subset.csv (parcels, pixels and tiles kept per round, for shares below 100%). report.py writes results/morocco/summary.csv, results/morocco/paired.csv and the figures e05_curves.png, e05_label_fraction.png and e05_confusion.png in docs/experiments/figures/.

    Learning curves¶

    100% of the labels; mean over the 5 rounds × 3 seeds, band = range.

    • Pretrained (frozen, LoRA, fine-tuned): validation macro-F1 is about 0.85 after the first epoch and close to its final value within 5–10 epochs. The training loss falls towards 0 while the validation loss stays flat and noisy (0.3–0.8): the head (and the adapters or encoder) fit the training pixels almost perfectly without further gain on new blocks. Fine-tuning and LoRA drive the training loss down faster than the frozen encoder, but do not reach a better validation score.
    • From scratch: the training loss falls slowly (0.6 to 0.2 in 60 epochs), and the validation macro-F1 climbs from about 0.55 with a wide spread between runs; some runs stay near the all-irrigated solution (about 0.47) for many epochs. The pretrained weights give the network a useful starting point that 300 M weights trained on these labels do not find.

    Interpretation¶

    1. How the strategies behave. The three pretrained strategies learn within a few epochs and then overfit the training pixels without losing validation score; training from scratch is slow and unstable between seeds.
    2. Cost and effect of training the encoder. LoRA (8 M weights) and full fine-tuning (306 M) cost 1.3–1.6 times the GPU time of the frozen encoder and change the score by less than 0.01 in either direction. Full fine-tuning shifts the errors (more precise, less complete for rainfed) and is weaker in the zone with few rainfed parcels.
    3. Whether fine-tuning wins: no, but pretraining does. At every share, the pretrained network beats the same network trained from scratch by 0.08–0.11 macro-F1 (0.14–0.20 rainfed F1). Frozen, LoRA and full fine-tuning cannot be told apart. This differs from Tomotaki-Dawoud et al. (2025), where full fine-tuning clearly beat the frozen encoder; possible reasons are the 10 m Sentinel-2 input close to Prithvi's pretraining data (they upsampled 30 m HLS to 0.5 m), the simpler two-class task, and the separate test fold and block bootstrap here.
    4. Fewer labels. The pretrained strategies hardly change from 100% down to 10% of the training parcels (527 parcels per round, of which 72 rainfed): 0.907–0.912 at 10% against 0.911–0.920 at 100%. With 10% of the parcels, the frozen encoder (0.907) still scores above the E01 random forest (0.894) and E03 U-TAE (0.900) trained on all parcels, though within their confidence intervals. From scratch, the network gains little from more parcels either (0.804 → 0.825): with at most 718 rainfed parcels per round, 300 M weights cannot be trained from random initialisation.
    5. For the maps: a frozen encoder with a light head is the cheapest strategy and as good as any; fine-tuning does not justify its cost on these labels.

    Limitations¶

    • One encoder, and Morocco first (Utah follows). Prithvi is pretrained on 30 m HLS and here receives 10 m Sentinel-2, so its frozen features meet a different pixel size than in pretraining; fine-tuning may gain more for Prithvi than for an encoder pretrained on Sentinel-2 at 10 m.
    • No search over learning rates per strategy, nor over the LoRA rank and adapted layers: a strategy may be favoured by settings that suit it better (Marti-Escofet et al. 2025 chose the learning rate of each method by a search).
    • The validation fold is complete at every share (see above).
    • Other parameter-efficient methods (adapters, prompt tuning) and two-stage linear probing then fine-tuning (LP-FT) are not compared (RQ2 · Fine-tuning).
