    E01 · Sentinel-2 pixel baseline¶

    Status: Morocco done (20 November 2026); Utah later

    Two classes (irrigated, rainfed) on the labelled pixels of the surveyed parcels: Morocco 2023/24 and 2024/25 pooled, 473,675 pixels of 8,775 parcels. Random forest macro-F1 0.894 [0.834, 0.940], rainfed F1 0.810; gradient boosting the same (0.893). Results

    Question. How well do the Sentinel-2 time series of single pixels separate irrigated from rainfed pixels? E01 is the reference for every later experiment: the paired tests compare each run with the E01 random forest.

    Why. Random forests on per-pixel time series are the standard in operational irrigation mapping (Ketchum et al. 2020; Xie et al. 2021). Any more complex model has to beat this reference on the same pixels.

    How E01 works, step by step¶

    E01 from start to end (schematic: the pictures illustrate the steps and show no results, so the figure holds for any region and year; tools/a9_experiment_explainers.py). Steps 4–6 repeat in each of the five rounds. The results of each region are under Results.

    1. Labelled pixels. The input is the table of pixel samples of the region: the inner pixels (at most 100 per parcel) of the surveyed parcels, with the years of the region pooled. Each pixel carries the survey label of its parcel and the fold of its 20 km block, so the pixels of one parcel always stay in the same fold.
    2. Features. Each pixel becomes one row of 168 numbers: for each of the 12 months of the agricultural year (the start month is set per region), its 12 Sentinel-2 bands plus NDVI and NDWI. The model sees these 168 values as separate columns; it does not know that they form a time series.
    3. Cloudy months. A month without a clear observation is filled by linear interpolation between the neighbouring clear months (first or last month: the nearest clear month; a band never observed: the median of the training pixels). How often this is needed depends on the region and year.
    4. Rotation over the folds. Five rounds: in round k, fold k is the test fold, fold k+1 the validation fold and the other three train the model. After five rounds every pixel has been tested once, by a model that never saw its block.
    5. Choosing the settings. In each round, every setting of the grid (4 for the random forest, 4 for gradient boosting) is trained on the three training folds and scored on the validation fold; the best one is kept. The test fold is never used for this choice.
    6. Predicting the test fold. The chosen model predicts the test fold. The five test folds together give one out-of-fold prediction for every pixel (oof_predictions.parquet), the same pixels for every experiment.
    7. Scores. The out-of-fold predictions of all pixels are compared with the survey labels: precision, recall and F1 of each class, macro-F1 (the mean of the two F1, the headline score, so that the rare rainfed class counts as much as irrigated), overall accuracy, and the same per year and zone.
    8. Uncertainty. The 20 km blocks of the region are drawn with replacement 1,000 times and macro-F1 is recomputed for each draw; the 2.5% and 97.5% values give the 95% confidence interval. Blocks are resampled rather than pixels because neighbouring pixels are not independent. Every experiment uses the same draws, so models are compared pair by pair on the same pixels.
    9. Importance. For the random forest, all values of one month (or of one band over all months) are shuffled between test pixels; the drop in macro-F1 shows how much the model relies on them.

    How the random forest works

    A decision tree splits the pixels step by step on single features ("NDVI in July > 0.45?", "B11 in March < 0.2?"), each time choosing the split that best separates the classes, until the groups are pure or small. One tree overfits easily. A random forest (Breiman 2001) grows many trees (here 500), each on a bootstrap sample of the training pixels and, at every split, on a random subset of the features (max_features: √168 ≈ 13 or 33%). The trees therefore make different mistakes, and the forest averages their votes into class probabilities. min_samples_leaf stops a tree from splitting groups smaller than 1 or 5 pixels.

    class_weight = "balanced" counts each rainfed pixel more, in inverse proportion to its share, so the forest does not simply favour the irrigated majority. The months are 168 separate features: the forest has no notion of their order, but it can still use any month that separates the classes.

    How histogram gradient boosting works

    Gradient boosting (Friedman 2001) also combines many trees, but builds them one after another: each new small tree is fitted to the errors (the gradient of the loss) of all trees before it, and its prediction is added with a small weight (the learning rate, 0.05 or 0.1). Many weak trees in sequence give a strong model. The histogram variant (as in LightGBM, Ke et al. 2017; scikit-learn HistGradientBoostingClassifier) first bins every feature into at most 255 value ranges, which makes training on hundreds of thousands of pixels fast. max_leaf_nodes (31 or 63) limits the size of each tree; training stops when an internal 10% validation split no longer improves for 20 iterations.

    How the permutation importance works

    After training, the values of one group of features are shuffled across the test pixels (e.g. all 14 values of July, or one band in all 12 months), which breaks their link to the label while keeping their distribution. The drop in macro-F1 compared with the unshuffled data is the importance of that group: a large drop means the forest relies on it. Grouping by month answers "which months matter", grouping by band "which bands matter".

    How to run¶

    Script: experiments/E01_s2_pixel_baseline/run.py (shared code: pixelwise_run/pixel_common.py).

    sbatch --export=ALL,SCRIPT=experiments/E01_s2_pixel_baseline/run.py,EXPERIMENT_REGION=morocco jobs/step_cpu.sbatch

    EXPERIMENT_QUICK_TEST=1 runs the whole chain on 20,000 pixels with one grid point (output in results_quick/). CPU only; the random-forest grid dominates the run time.

    Results¶

    Morocco, 2023/24 and 2024/25 pooled: 473,675 out-of-fold pixels (419,883 irrigated, 53,792 rainfed). Every pixel is predicted once, by the model of the round in which its fold was the test fold. The comparison with the other experiments is on the comparison page.

    Scores¶

    Model Macro-F1 [95% CI] Irrigated F1 Rainfed F1 [95% CI] Rainfed precision Rainfed recall Overall accuracy
    Random forest (reference) 0.894 [0.834, 0.940] 0.977 0.810 [0.697, 0.894] 0.850 0.774 0.959
    Gradient boosting 0.893 [0.822, 0.946] 0.977 0.809 [0.673, 0.903] 0.850 0.773 0.959

    95% confidence intervals from the block bootstrap over the 20 km blocks. The two models are practically identical: gradient boosting minus random forest is −0.000 macro-F1 [−0.017, 0.014] (paired test, Holm-adjusted p = 1.0 over the nine runs of E01–E04).

    Row-normalised confusion matrices (share of the reference pixels of each class; pixel counts in brackets).

    Errors are one-sided. 98% of the irrigated pixels are classified correctly, but 23% of the rainfed pixels (12,166 of 53,792) are classified as irrigated. Rainfed precision (0.85) is higher than rainfed recall (0.77): when the random forest says "rainfed" it is mostly right, but it misses about one rainfed pixel in four.

    Doukkala 46,608 0.843 0.858 0.864
    Souss 6,869 0.322 0.485 0.363
    Statte 312 0.458 0.485 0.526
    • The rainfed class comes almost entirely from Doukkala. 87% of the rainfed pixels (1,122 of 1,196 rainfed parcels) lie in Doukkala, 13% in Souss and Statte; Gharb and Tadla have no rainfed parcel and el Haouz one. In the three irrigation-only zones the classifier marks almost every pixel irrigated (accuracy ≥ 0.985), so the rainfed score of E01 is in practice a score of Doukkala and, to a lesser extent, Souss.
    • 2024/25 is harder for rainfed. Rainfed recall falls from 0.86 (2023/24) to 0.66 (2024/25), while rainfed precision rises to 0.96. In 2024/25 more rainfed pixels are taken for irrigated. A plausible reason is the rainfall of the two years: 2023/24 was a drought year, while in a wetter winter rainfed cereals stay green longer and look more like irrigated fields in the winter months. This is an interpretation; the rainfall of the two years has not been checked here.
    • Souss is the weakest zone. Only 32% of the rainfed pixels of Souss are found (gradient boosting: 22%). Its 69 rainfed parcels are few, and the random forest learns rainfed mostly from Doukkala.

    Per test fold. Macro-F1 of the random forest per test fold: 0.762 (fold 0), 0.911, 0.921, 0.942, 0.928 (folds 1–4). Fold 0 is the hardest: it holds the 5,869 rainfed pixels of Souss in 2024/25, of which only 31% are found, and 2,617 irrigated pixels of Doukkala classified as rainfed. Round 5, which validates on fold 0, also had the lowest validation scores (0.73–0.76), so the spread between folds is a property of the data, not of the model.

    Which months and bands matter¶

    Drop in macro-F1 when the features of one month (left) or one band over all months (right) are shuffled; mean and standard deviation over the 5 rounds.

    • Months: February (0.17), July (0.17), August (0.15), June (0.12) and January (0.11) matter most; March and November hardly at all (≤ 0.03). The winter peak (January–February) is when rainfed cereals and irrigated crops are both green but differ in vigour; the summer months (June–August) are when rainfed fields are bare and many irrigated fields still carry a crop or orchard.
    • Bands: the two shortwave-infrared bands dominate: B12 (0.24) and B11 (0.20), then NDVI (0.11) and the red-edge band B7 (0.10). SWIR responds to the water content of soil and vegetation, which is what irrigation changes. The visible bands B3, B4 and B5 add almost nothing once the other bands are known (correlated bands share their importance).

    Interpretation¶

    • A pixel classifier on the Sentinel-2 time series already reaches macro-F1 0.89 for irrigated vs rainfed in Morocco. Every later model is measured against this reference on the same pixels.
    • Random forest and gradient boosting give the same answer, so the result reflects the information in the input, not the choice of tree ensemble.
    • The weak point is the rainfed class outside Doukkala and in the wetter year 2024/25: the errors are rainfed pixels taken for irrigated. Whether temporal or spatial context (E02–E04) helps is tested on these pixels.
    • Because rainfed parcels exist in only three of six zones, part of what any model learns may be "which zone" rather than "how the crop behaves". The per-zone scores are reported for every experiment to keep this visible.
