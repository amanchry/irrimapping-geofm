    Comparison of the experiments¶

    Status: Morocco done, all runs of E01–E04 (22 November 2026); Utah later

    Morocco answer to RQ1: frozen foundation models give the best scores (TerraMind 0.927 macro-F1) and are significantly better than the same pipeline trained from scratch (U-TAE), but not significantly better than a random forest on the pixel time series. This page holds all runs of E01–E04 side by side. Each experiment page reports only its own runs; E05 (Prithvi frozen / LoRA / fine-tuned / from scratch) is compared within itself on its own page.

    What is compared¶

    All runs of a region are scored on the same out-of-fold pixels: Morocco 2023/24 and 2024/25 pooled, 473,675 labelled pixels of 8,775 surveyed parcels, two classes (irrigated, rainfed).

    Run Experiment Model
    E01 random forest E01 pixel time series, random forest (reference)
    E01 gradient boosting E01 pixel time series, histogram gradient boosting
    E02 TempCNN E02 pixel time series, temporal CNN
    E03 U-TAE E03 whole tiles, trained from scratch (reference for E04)
    E04 Prithvi, Clay, TerraMind, AgriFM, Presto, TESSERA E04 whole tiles, frozen foundation model + L-TAE + UPerNet

    Answers to the RQ1 questions (Morocco)¶

    Question Answer Evidence
    Q1 · Do frozen GeoFMs beat a classifier on the raw Sentinel-2 time series? Not significantly. All six score above the random forest (+0.009 to +0.034 macro-F1); TerraMind's CI excludes 0 but not after the Holm correction E04
    Q2 · Do they beat a network trained from scratch on the same tiles? Yes, for the four image encoders (TerraMind, Clay, AgriFM, Prithvi: +0.017 to +0.027, significant); not for Presto and TESSERA E04
    Q3 · Does a temporal neural network beat the random forest? Not significantly (TempCNN +0.018 [−0.017, +0.053]) E02
    Q4 · Image models or pixel time-series models? Image models score higher (0.917–0.927 vs 0.903–0.912) and are 3–14 times faster here; the four image encoders cannot be ranked among themselves E04
    Q5 · Second region Utah, after Morocco –

    What the results show¶

    • All models are within 0.034 macro-F1 of each other (0.893–0.927). The separation of irrigated and rainfed pixels is largely possible from the Sentinel-2 time series of a single pixel; the shortwave-infrared bands and the winter and summer months carry most of it (E01).
    • The differences are in the hard cases: rainfed fields in the wetter year 2024/25 and in Souss, where few rainfed parcels exist. Rainfed recall in Souss rises from 0.32 (random forest) to 0.64–0.68 (TempCNN, U-TAE) and 0.81–0.84 (TerraMind, Clay); in 2024/25 from 0.66 to 0.85.
    • Pretrained features help beyond spatial context: with tiles, labels and head identical, the pretrained image encoders beat U-TAE trained from scratch. E05 confirms this for Prithvi (pretrained vs from scratch: +0.08 to +0.11 at every share of the labels) and shows that fine-tuning the encoder adds nothing over keeping it frozen.
    • Why the comparisons with the random forest stay inconclusive: the rainfed pixels lie in few 20 km blocks, mostly in Doukkala, and the gains of the tile models come from a few of them (fold 0: 0.76 → 0.86–0.88), while in fold 1 the random forest is best. The block bootstrap, which treats the block as the unit, therefore gives wide intervals. Utah, with rainfed land spread more widely, will test whether the ranking holds.
