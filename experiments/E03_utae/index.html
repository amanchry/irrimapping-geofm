    E03 · U-TAE from scratch¶

    Status: planned (Morocco first, Utah later)

    Same labelled pixels as E01 (Morocco 2023/24 and 2024/25 pooled, 473,675 pixels in 2,015 tiles; two classes), but the model sees the whole 128 px tile; 3 seeds per round on one GPU. Code ready and tested; results follow after the run.

    Question. Does a spatio-temporal network trained from scratch on the whole tile beat the pixel models (E01, E02)? It is also the reference for the frozen foundation models of E04: it shows how much the pretrained encoders add beyond spatial context.

    What changes from E02. The model sees the whole 128 px tile (spatial context) instead of one pixel. The labelled pixels, folds and metrics are the same.

    Model¶

    U-TAE (Garnot & Landrieu 2021), the PASTIS semantic-segmentation baseline: a convolutional encoder shared by all months, a lightweight temporal attention encoder (L-TAE) at the coarsest resolution that produces one attention mask per head and pixel, these masks aggregate the skip features of every resolution over time, and a convolutional decoder brings the result back to 10 m.

    Item Choice
    Input 12 Sentinel-2 bands × 12 months of the whole tile, standardised per band with the training pixels of the round; missing values 0, months without any clear pixel masked in the attention
    Widths encoder 64–64–64–128, decoder 32–32–64–128 (the PASTIS configuration)
    Temporal attention L-TAE at 1/8 resolution: 16 heads, dk 4, dmodel 256
    Output 2 class scores (irrigated, rainfed) for every 10 m pixel of the tile
    Parameters 1.15 M, all trained from random weights
    How U-TAE works

    U-TAE (U-Net with Temporal Attention Encoder; Garnot & Landrieu 2021) classifies every pixel of an image time series. It combines a U-Net, which sees the neighbourhood of each pixel at several resolutions, with temporal attention, which decides which months matter at each place.

    flowchart TD A["tile 128 × 128 px · 12 months · 12 bands"] --> B["CNN encoder, the same for every month:<br/>maps at 128², 64², 32², 16² px"] B --> C["L-TAE on the 16² map:<br/>month weights per position (16 heads)"] C --> D["month weights upsampled and applied at every size:<br/>12 months → one map per size"] D --> E["U-Net decoder: 16² → 128²,<br/>adding the map of each size (skip connections)"] E --> F["2 class scores per 10 m pixel"]
    1. Encoder per month. A small convolutional network describes the image of each month on its own (the same weights for all 12 months) and halves the resolution three times: 128, 64, 32 and 16 px.
    2. Temporal attention at the coarsest level. At each position of the 16 × 16 map, an L-TAE looks at the 12 months and gives each one a weight (per head), from the data of that position: months without a clear observation get no weight.
    3. The same month weights at every level. The weights are upsampled and used at all four resolutions to collapse the 12 months into one map per resolution.
    4. Decoder. Starting from the coarsest map, the decoder upsamples step by step and adds the map of the same resolution from step 3 (skip connections), so the result keeps the 10 m detail of the image while using the context of the coarser maps.

    Everything is trained from random weights on our labels only; it is the counterpart of E04 without pretraining.

    How L-TAE works (temporal attention)

    L-TAE (Lightweight Temporal Attention Encoder; Garnot & Landrieu 2020) turns a sequence of 12 monthly feature vectors into one vector. Each month gets a code for its position in the agricultural year; each of the 16 heads has a learnt master query and scores every month against it; a softmax turns the scores into weights that sum to 1 (months without data get weight 0); each head takes the weighted average of the months, and the heads are joined. Unlike a convolution, the weights depend on the data of each pixel or position. The same module is the temporal head of the foundation models in E04 and E05.

    Training¶

    Item Choice
    Labels the labelled pixels of the round's training folds; every other pixel of the tile is ignored in the loss. A tile with pixels of two folds contributes only its training-fold labels
    Loss class-weighted cross-entropy (weights from the training pixels: rainfed counts about 8 times more)
    Optimiser AdamW, learning rate 10⁻³, weight decay 10⁻⁴, batch 4 tiles
    Augmentation random flips and 90° rotations
    Early stopping macro-F1 of the validation pixels, patience 10, at most 100 epochs
    Seeds 3 per round (42, 1042, 2042), mean probability
    Scoring the test tiles are predicted and read at their labelled pixels: the same out-of-fold pixels as every other experiment

    How to run¶

    Script: experiments/E03_utae/run.py (model and training loop: pixelwise_run/tile_common.py).

    sbatch --export=ALL,SCRIPT=experiments/E03_utae/run.py,EXPERIMENT_REGION=morocco jobs/step_gpu.sbatch

    EXPERIMENT_QUICK_TEST=1 runs the chain on 60 tiles for 2 epochs (output in results_quick/; too short to learn the rainfed class, it only checks the code).

    Outputs¶

    experiments/E03_utae/results/<region>/utae/: the E01 files plus training_history.csv (per epoch: training loss, validation loss, validation macro-F1 and rainfed F1, seconds).
