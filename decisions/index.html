    Decisions and departures from the proposal¶

    This page records every departure of the implementation from the research proposal, stating the original plan, the current approach and the rationale. Smaller method choices are in the progress log.

    D1. Spatial folds instead of a 70/30 split¶

    • Proposal (§4.1): stratified 70 / 30 split, validation subset from the training data. (§4.2.4 already asks for spatial cross-validation.)
    • Now: parcels grouped into 20 km blocks, blocks into 5 folds with equal class balance; every parcel tested once by a model that never saw its block.
    • Why: with a random split, 79% of test parcels lie within 250 m of a training parcel (about 2% with 20 km blocks). Random splits overstate accuracy for mapping new areas (Roberts et al. 2017; Ploton et al. 2020).
    • Where: dataset §5, protocol §2.

    D2. Two agricultural years (per-parcel season)¶

    • Proposal (§4.1): the 2024 annual image stack.
    • Now: each parcel gets the 12 months (1 Sep – 31 Aug) of the season in which it was surveyed: 2023/24 or 2024/25. Both seasons in one dataset, months aligned (month 1 = September).
    • Why: the survey ran Dec 2023 – Jan 2025; using only 2024 would mix seasons, and dropping 2024/25 would lose 42% of the rainfed parcels. The agricultural year covers the winter rainfed cycle and the summer, when only irrigated fields stay green.
    • Consequence: region and year are partly confounded (Gharb and Tadla only 2023/24, Souss only 2024/25).
    • Where: dataset §6.2.

    D3. Validation: PASTIS rotation instead of nested CV¶

    • Before (E01): nested spatial CV, settings by inner CV on the 4 training folds, refit on 4.
    • Now: per round 3 folds train, the next fold chooses the settings (or stops training), 1 fold tests, as in PASTIS (Garnot & Landrieu 2021).
    • Why: one design for classical classifiers and neural networks (L-TAE, fine-tuning), which need a separate validation fold for early stopping. It is the standard design of the PASTIS crop-mapping benchmark.
    • Where: protocol §2; E01 is kept as the sensitivity check.

    D4. One shared tile store for every input¶

    • Proposal: annual Sentinel-1/2 composites via STAC and xarray.
    • Now: Google Earth Engine builds non-overlapping 128 × 128 px tiles (1.28 km) with 12 monthly composites × 19 bands; the baseline features and the foundation-model chips are all cut from these tiles.
    • Why: STAC processing was too slow for the full set; with one store every input sees identical pixels, compositing and masks, and chips no longer overlap across folds.
    • Where: dataset §6–7.

    D5. Frozen encoder + classical classifiers¶

    • Proposal (§4.2.1): frozen encoder, train a decoder and classification head.
    • Now: the task is per parcel, so the frozen encoder gives one embedding per parcel and the "head" is a classifier (linear probe and three others). Fine-tuning (LoRA, RQ2) follows as planned.
    • Why: tests what the pretrained representation already contains, at equal cost for every model; the linear probe is the standard test of frozen features.

    D6. Four classifiers instead of one Random Forest¶

    • Proposal: Random Forest baseline.
    • Now: logistic regression, random forest, gradient boosting and SVM on every input (RF stays the headline).
    • Why: a classifier that suits hand-built features (RF) may not suit dense embeddings (E01: RF is the weakest classifier on embeddings). Reporting all four keeps the comparison from depending on one choice.

    D7. Macro-F1 as the headline metric¶

    • Proposal (§4.2.4): OA, precision, recall, F1; best model by mean F1 over the five folds, SD as consistency.
    • Now: macro-F1 of all out-of-fold predictions with a 95% block-bootstrap CI as headline; per-fold mean ± SD is reported too, as the proposal asked.
    • Why: with 86% irrigated parcels, overall accuracy and irrigated F1 are high for any model; macro-F1 weights the rainfed class equally. Paired bootstrap differences say whether a gap is real.

    D8. Two temporal aggregation pipelines¶

    • Proposal: not specified.
    • Now: PCA pipeline (concat 12 months → PCA 128) in E01 / E02; L-TAE pipeline in E03.
    • Why: PANGAEA (Marsocci et al. 2024) found L-TAE better than linear mapping for single-date encoders; testing both shows whether the conclusion depends on how the months are combined.
    • Where: protocol §5.

    D9. Cloud Score+ instead of QA60¶

    • Now: Sentinel-2 pixels kept when Cloud Score+ cs_cdf ≥ 0.60; scenes with up to 60% cloud admitted.
    • Why: the QA60 band misses thin cloud and shadow; Cloud Score+ is the recommended mask in Earth Engine (Pasquarella et al. 2023).

    D10. Transfer region under review¶

    • Proposal (§3.2): zero-shot transfer to Tunisia, validated on 54 GMIA-NEXT points (44 irrigated, 10 rainfed).
    • Now: Morocco first. As an alternative, US state datasets with both irrigated and dryland fields (eastern Washington WSDA, Utah WRLU 2017–2025) would allow a spatial and a multi-year test with the same pipeline.
    • Why: 10 rainfed points are too few to measure a rainfed F1; the US data needs a check of its labels (see the progress log, 4–5 Nov 2026).
    • Decision due: after RQ1 and RQ2.

    D11. Two kinds of head: parcel benchmark and mapping model¶

    • Proposal (§4.2.1, §4.3): frozen encoder with a decoder / head; annual irrigated maps 2021–2025 for Morocco.
    • Problem: the RQ1 pipeline pools the embeddings over the parcel polygon. Annual maps of Morocco (or of a transfer region) have no polygons, and the GeoFM patches (80 m Clay, 160 m the others) are too coarse for parcels of 0.83 ha (median, about 90 m across).
    • Now:
    • RQ1 benchmark (unchanged): frozen encoder → parcel embedding → LogReg (linear probe), RF, HGB, SVM; plus L-TAE + MLP head (E03). Classical heads on frozen embeddings are common practice (Presto; WorldCereal; AlphaEarth irrigated mapping, Yang et al. 2026) and the head matters little in recent comparisons (Mughees et al. 2026).
    • Mapping models (RQ2–RQ4): frozen GeoFM + UPerNet segmentation head trained on the label pixels of the tiles (as in PANGAEA and Prithvi-EO-2.0), then LoRA with the same head (RQ2). Classical baseline: RF on the S1/S2 features of each pixel.
    • Neural baselines from scratch: TempCNN (per-pixel time series; Pelletier et al. 2019) and U-TAE (tiles; Garnot & Landrieu 2021). Both produce 10 m maps. PSE-TAE needs parcel polygons and stays a parcel-level extra (E04).
    • Same scoring: pixel predictions are aggregated per parcel (mean probability) and scored with the same folds, parcels and bootstrap as RQ1, so every number stays comparable.
    • Why: the research must end in wall-to-wall annual maps; a 1D-CNN beat RF for irrigated mapping (Bazzi et al. 2019) and TempCNN-type models transferred better across regions than GeoFM embeddings (Tong & Wang 2025), so a neural baseline is needed next to the classical one.
    • Where: literature notes.

    D12. AgriFM with its pretrained 4-px patch embedding¶

    • Before: 02d_embed_agrifm.py built the Sentinel-2 encoder with the patch size of the repository's fine-tuning configuration (configs/cropland_mapping.py: 4 time steps × 2 × 2 px, output stride 16 px) and loaded AgriFM.pth through mmengine. No weight was loaded: the checkpoint names carry the prefix encoder., and the pretrained patch embedding (S2_patch_emd, kernel 128 × 10 × 4 × 4 × 4) does not fit a 2 × 2 px patch. mmengine only warns about mismatched names and still reports success, so all AgriFM embeddings produced up to 6 Nov 2026 (local and on the cluster, including E01) came from a randomly initialised encoder.
    • Now: patch size 4 × 4 × 4 (the size of the pretrained patch embedding), output stride 32 px (320 m; 2 × 2 cells per 64-px chip). The weights are mapped by name in the script and every encoder tensor must load; otherwise the script stops. On the cluster 349 of 349 encoder tensors load.
    • Why: the frozen-encoder comparison (D5) must use each encoder as pretrained. In the fine-tuning set-up of the repository the 2 × 2 px patch embedding is trained anew; with a frozen encoder it would stay random. The alternative, resizing the 4 × 4 px kernel to 2 × 2 px, keeps a finer grid but replaces the pretrained first layer by an approximation.
    • Consequence: AgriFM has the coarsest grid of the four models (320 m against 80–160 m), which matters for small parcels; the patch-share weighting is unchanged. Earlier AgriFM results are void and are re-run.
    • Where: models_run/02d_embed_agrifm.py, Foundation models · inputs.
