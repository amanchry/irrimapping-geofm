    RQ1 · Frozen encoders: embeddings and classifiers¶

    This page documents the frozen-encoder evaluation of Phase 2 (RQ1), which tests whether frozen embeddings of geospatial foundation models (GeoFMs) discriminate irrigated from rainfed cropland parcels in Morocco better than a conventional Sentinel-2 feature baseline under an identical spatial validation design. Every input uses Sentinel-2 only (D14).

    It reads only the ML-ready dataset produced in ml_dataset_prep/. The rules of the comparison are summarised in the evaluation protocol; reported results are produced as experiments, which run these scripts with the settings of a config file.

    2. Research questions and design¶

    1. Main: does a frozen GeoFM embedding classify irrigated vs rainfed parcels better than Sentinel-1/2 parcel features, when tested on areas the model has never seen?
    2. Which GeoFM works best, and does the classifier on top matter?
    3. How much does random cross-validation overstate accuracy compared with spatial cross-validation?
    4. Which months (and, for the baseline, which variables) carry the signal?

    The design keeps everything equal except the input:

    flowchart LR SAME["<b>Kept identical for every input</b><br/>same parcels (intersection of all runs)<br/>same pixels (one tile store)<br/>same spatial folds (20 km blocks)<br/>same 4 classifiers and grids<br/>same metrics and bootstrap samples"] --> D["<b>Only the input differs</b><br/>S1/S2 features vs<br/>Clay · Prithvi · TerraMind · AgriFM"] D --> C["<b>Paired difference</b><br/>in macro-F1 with<br/>95% block-bootstrap CI"]

    The foundation models are used frozen, as fixed feature extractors, following the linear-probing protocol. No fine-tuning is performed at this stage; the evaluation therefore measures the information contained in the pretrained representations, at low computational cost and under identical conditions for all inputs.

    The five inputs (Sentinel-2 baseline and four frozen GeoFMs, all Sentinel-2 only), their bands, patch sizes and temporal handling, and the optional Sentinel-1 ablation are specified in Foundation models · Configuration.

    4. Script 01 — inspect_dataset¶

    Purpose: find problems in the data before they show up as strange model results. It reads the dataset only and changes nothing.

    flowchart LR IN["<b>Dataset files</b><br/>parcels · tiles · overlaps<br/>parcel features · chips · views_info"] --> S["<b>Seven checks</b><br/>1 · inventory per class, season, fold<br/>2 · label QA: overlaps, tiny parcels<br/>3 · observations per month (S2, S1)<br/>4 · patch purity, aligned vs centred<br/>5 · seasonal profiles per class<br/>6 · example chips, 12 months<br/>7 · agreement with the old NDVI table"] S --> OUT["outputs/morocco/study/<br/>01_inspection/"]

    What to look for: - Inventory: all 8,781 parcels should reach view A and both chip folders. Missing parcels point to tiles that failed to download. - Observations: winter months (Dec–Feb) have the fewest clear Sentinel-2 views. If a month is empty for most parcels, its features carry little information. - Purity: the aligned chips should have a clearly higher median purity than the centred chips, especially for 16-px patches and small parcels. - Profiles: rainfed parcels should peak in winter (around February) and be bare from May. Irrigated parcels should stay green, or green up again, in summer. This is the signal every model should find. - Legacy agreement: high per-month correlation with the old Earth Engine NDVI confirms the new compositing. The old table used 30-day periods rather than calendar months, so the numbers will not be identical.

    Outputs: - inventory.csv - label_qa.csv - observations_per_month.csv / .png - purity_summary.csv, purity.png - seasonal_profiles.png - examples_irrigated.png, examples_rainfed.png - legacy_agreement.csv

    The resulting figures are shown in Dataset preparation §7.6.

    For a closer look in QGIS, tools/a3_export_chips_geotiff.py writes single chips as georeferenced GeoTIFFs to 01_inspection/chips_geotiff/.

    5. Scripts 02a–02d — foundation-model embeddings¶

    All four scripts follow the same pattern. Only the model, its bands and its normalisation change.

    flowchart TD A["Chip of one parcel<br/>12 months × 15 bands × 64 × 64 px<br/>+ parcel mask"] --> B["Select the bands the model<br/>was pretrained on"] B --> C["Normalise per band: the model's pretraining<br/>mean / std (AgriFM: the region's chip statistics)<br/>NODATA (cloud) → 0 = band mean"] C --> D["Frozen encoder<br/>(no gradient, eval mode)"] D --> E["Patch tokens on their grid<br/>8 × 8 (Clay), 4 × 4 (Prithvi, TerraMind), 2 × 2 (AgriFM)"] A -->|"mask"| W["Patch weight = share of the<br/>patch's pixels inside the parcel"] E --> F["Weighted mean of the patches<br/>covering the parcel"] W --> F F --> G["One vector per month<br/>(AgriFM: one per year)"] G --> H["valid flag = month had<br/>any clear pixel"] G --> O["&lt;model&gt;_agyear_tiles.npz<br/>embeddings [N, steps, D] float16"] H --> O

    Pooling. Each patch token is weighted by the share of its pixels inside the parcel. Patches without any parcel pixel get weight 0. The parcel embedding is the weighted mean, so it describes the parcel and not the whole 640 m chip. The patch-aligned chips (dataset §7.3) put most of the weight on one patch centred on the parcel.

    Cloudy pixels. NODATA pixels are set to 0 after normalisation, which equals the band mean. A month with no clear pixel at all is flagged valid = False, and script 03 fills it with the training median.

    Resume. Embeddings are saved in batches (_parts_<model>_agyear_tiles/) and merged at the end. A stopped run continues from the last batch.

    Script Model Output
    02a_embed_clay.py Clay v1.5 [N, 12, 1024]

    5.2 How each model handles the 12 months¶

    The four models treat time differently. Clay, Prithvi and TerraMind return one embedding per month (12 per parcel), and this study decides how the months are combined (PCA in E02 / E03, L-TAE in E04). AgriFM merges the months inside the network and returns one embedding for the whole year.

    Model Months per forward pass Passes per parcel Time steps out per pass Per parcel Months merged inside the model?
    Clay v1.5 1 12 1 12 × 1,024 no (single-date model)
    TerraMind 1 12 1 12 × 768 no (single-date model)
    Prithvi-EO-2.0 4 (FRAMES_PER_PASS = 4, as in its pretraining) 3 4 12 × 1,024 no: months only share context
    AgriFM 12 (the whole year) 1 1 1 × 1,024 yes: averaged down to one time step
    flowchart LR subgraph SD["Clay · TerraMind (single date)"] direction TB S1["month 1"] --> SE1["encoder"] --> SO1["embedding 1"] S2["…"] --> SE2["…"] --> SO2["…"] S12["month 12"] --> SE12["encoder"] --> SO12["embedding 12"] end subgraph PR["Prithvi (4 months per pass)"] direction TB P1["Sep–Dec"] --> PE1["encoder"] --> PO1["4 embeddings<br/>Sep · Oct · Nov · Dec"] P2["Jan–Apr"] --> PE2["encoder"] --> PO2["4 embeddings"] P3["May–Aug"] --> PE3["encoder"] --> PO3["4 embeddings"] end subgraph AG["AgriFM (whole year)"] direction TB A1["12 months"] --> AE["encoder:<br/>12 → 3 → 2 → 1 time steps"] --> AO["1 embedding<br/>for the year"] end

    Clay and TerraMind are single-date models: each monthly chip is encoded on its own, so every month gets its own embedding and the months never see each other.

    Prithvi-EO-2.0 is multi-temporal, but it does not merge the months. It was pretrained on 4 dates per sample, so the script gives it 4 consecutive months per pass: Sep–Dec, Jan–Apr and May–Aug in Morocco (Oct–Jan, Feb–May, Jun–Sep in Utah). Its tokens span 1 month × 16 × 16 px (temporal patch size 1) and no layer reduces the time axis, so 4 months in give 4 sets of tokens out: for a 64 px chip, 4 months × a 4 × 4 patch grid ([B, T, n, n, D] with T = 4 in 02b_embed_prithvi.py). The only effect of passing 4 months together is attention between them: each month's embedding is computed with the other three as context. Three passes × 4 months = 12 embeddings. They could be averaged to one per pass, but they are kept monthly so that the months are combined in the same way as for Clay and TerraMind.

    AgriFM is built like a video model (modified Video Swin Transformer; Li et al. 2025) and compresses time inside the network, as it compresses space. In the encoder code (external/AgriFM/AgriFM/models/video_swin_transformer.py):

    1. The pretrained patch embedding covers 4 months × 4 × 4 px (D12), so 12 months become 3 time steps.
    2. With mean_frame_down = True, each downsampling stage also averages neighbouring time steps (padding an odd count): 3 → 2 → 1.
    3. The output (feature_fusion = "cat") folds any remaining time steps into the channels; with one step left this gives 1,024 channels.

    The script receives a feature map of 1,024 channels on a 2 × 2 grid (320 m cells for a 64 px chip), pools the cells covering the parcel and stores it as [N, 1, 1024] with the single step "year".

    Consequences for the experiments

    Clay · Prithvi · TerraMind AgriFM
    Who combines the months this study (PCA or L-TAE) the pretrained network
    Input to PCA (E02 / E03) 12 × D = 9,216–12,288 values → PCA 128 1,024 values → PCA 128 (same input size for the head)
    L-TAE (E04) yes not applicable: no sequence of months
    Which months matter measurable (permutation importance in E02, attention weights in E04) not measurable: no per-month embeddings
    valid flag per month (clear pixels in that month) one flag: at least one observed month

    Limitation to report. AgriFM was pretrained on dense time series (5-day steps; its configuration uses 32 frames). Here it receives 12 monthly composites, so each of its time tokens spans 4 months instead of about 20 days. The weights load and the input shape is valid, but the model's fine temporal resolution is not used; this is reported next to the Prithvi limitation (pretrained on 30 m HLS, applied to 10 m Sentinel-2).

    Purpose: one script for every input, so all inputs are evaluated in exactly the same way. Set INPUT at the top and run it once per input.

    6.1 From input to feature matrix¶

    flowchart TD subgraph BASE["INPUT = s1s2 (baseline)"] A1["parcel_features_inner.csv"] --> A2["Drop bookkeeping and count columns<br/>keep one S1 orbit, renamed VV / VH"] A2 --> A3["Fill fully cloudy months by<br/>linear interpolation in time<br/>(per parcel, own values only)"] A3 --> A4["180 features<br/>15 variables × 12 months (Sentinel-2)"] end subgraph EMB["INPUT = GeoFM"] B1["&lt;model&gt;_agyear_tiles.npz"] --> B2{"FEATURE_MODE"} B2 -->|"concat (default)"| B3["all months side by side<br/>e.g. 12 × 1024 = 12,288"] B2 -->|"mean"| B4["average over months"] end A4 --> J["Join with folds (parcels_folds.geojson)<br/>keep parcels present in both"] B3 --> J B4 --> J J --> X["X, y, fold, block_id"]

    Preprocessing inside every model pipeline. It is fitted on the training folds only, so no information leaks from the test fold: - Baseline: median imputation of any remaining gap. - Embeddings: median imputation of empty months, then standardisation. In concat mode, PCA to 128 components follows.

    6.2 Classifiers and hyperparameter grids¶

    Name Classifier Principle Grid (chosen on the validation fold) Fixed settings
    logreg Logistic regression one linear decision boundary; on frozen embeddings the standard linear probe (Alain & Bengio, 2016), which measures the information contained in the features C ∈ L2 penalty, lbfgs, max_iter = 5000, class_weight = "balanced", standardised inputs
    rf Random forest (Breiman, 2001) vote of many decision trees, each grown on a bootstrap sample and random feature subsets; the usual classifier in irrigation mapping max_features ∈ {√p, 0.3}; min_samples_leaf ∈ {1, 5}; class_weight ∈ {none, balanced_subsample} 500 trees, no depth limit, seed 42
    svm RBF support vector machine maximum-margin boundary, made non-linear by the RBF kernel; used for irrigation mapping with Sentinel-1/2 (Gao et al., 2018; Elwan et al., 2022) C ∈ {1, 10}; γ ∈ {scale, 0.01} class_weight = "balanced", standardised inputs; no probability calibration (the decision score is used for PR-AUC)

    Histogram gradient boosting was a fourth classifier in E01 and is dropped from E02 on (D6). The settings selected in each round are reported per experiment (E01).

    The selection score is macro-F1, the mean of the irrigated F1 and rainfed F1. It treats the 14% rainfed class as equally important.

    6.3 Spatial cross-validation: train / validation / test¶

    Each round uses the 5 spatial folds in the same way as the PASTIS benchmark (Garnot & Landrieu 2021): 3 folds train, the next fold is the validation fold, and one fold is the test fold. Rotating through 5 rounds tests every fold once.

    Round Train Validation Test
    1 folds 2, 3, 4 fold 1 fold 0
    2 folds 3, 4, 0 fold 2 fold 1
    3 folds 4, 0, 1 fold 3 fold 2
    4 folds 0, 1, 2 fold 4 fold 3
    5 folds 1, 2, 3 fold 0 fold 4
    flowchart TD START["5 spatial folds<br/>(20 km blocks)"] --> R{"Round k = 1 … 5"} R --> TE["Test = fold k<br/>held out completely"] R --> VA["Validation = next fold<br/>chooses the hyperparameters"] R --> TR["Train = the other 3 folds"] TR --> G["Train every setting of the grid"] G --> VA2["Score each setting on the validation fold (macro-F1)"] VA --> VA2 VA2 --> BEST["Best setting, trained on the 3 training folds"] BEST --> PRED["Predict test fold k<br/>(class + rainfed score)"] TE --> PRED PRED --> OOF["After 5 rounds: every parcel predicted once,<br/>by a model that never saw its block"] OOF --> MET["Metrics + 95% block-bootstrap CI"]

    The validation fold is used only to choose the classifier's hyperparameters; the test fold is never used for any choice. The same scheme is used for every input, so the classifiers and a later neural-network baseline are trained, tuned and tested identically. VALIDATION = "nested" in 03_train_evaluate.py gives the alternative design (tuning by spatial CV on the 4 training folds, refit on all 4), for a sensitivity check; its runs get the suffix _nested.

    Every parcel is predicted once, by a model that saw neither its block in training nor any information from it during tuning. That makes the score an estimate of how well the model maps new areas.

    6.4 Metrics and uncertainty¶

    Metric Why
    Macro-F1 (headline) balances both classes; insensitive to the 86 / 14 split
    F1, precision, recall per class rainfed recall shows how many rainfed parcels are found
    Balanced accuracy mean recall of both classes
    Overall accuracy for comparison with the literature; the trivial "all irrigated" model already gets 86%
    PR-AUC (rainfed) threshold-free ranking quality for the minority class
    Brier score, expected calibration error quality of the predicted probabilities

    95% confidence intervals: block bootstrap. Parcels in one 20 km block are correlated, so blocks, not parcels, are the independent units. Each of the 1,000 draws resamples whole blocks with replacement and recomputes every metric. The 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles give the interval.

    flowchart LR A["Out-of-fold predictions<br/>with block_id"] --> B["Draw 91 blocks<br/>with replacement"] B --> C["Recompute metrics on<br/>the parcels of those blocks"] C --> D{"1,000 draws?"} D -->|no| B D -->|yes| E["2.5% / 97.5% quantiles<br/>= 95% CI"]

    6.5 Further analyses in the same run¶

    1. Classifier comparison: paired macro-F1 differences between the 3 classifiers on the same parcels, with block-bootstrap CIs.
    2. Spatial vs random CV: the same hyperparameters re-evaluated with the stratified random_fold. The gap shows how much random validation overstates accuracy.
    3. Grouped permutation importance: uses the random forest of each outer fold, on its test fold. For the baseline it is computed by variable (NDVI, VH, …) and by month; for embeddings, by month.
    4. Seasonal profiles (baseline): class medians per variable and month.
    5. Final models: refit on all parcels (final_model_<name>.joblib), for mapping later.

    Steps 2, 3 and 5 are skipped in label-efficiency runs (§6.6), which only need the out-of-fold predictions.

    Outputs (experiments/<EXPERIMENT>/results/<REGION>/<RUN_NAME>/ in experiment mode; outputs/<REGION>/study/03_models/<RUN_NAME>/ for development runs with EXPERIMENT = None): - metrics_summary.csv, metrics_with_ci.csv, metrics_per_fold.csv - paired_model_differences.csv - cv_random_vs_spatial.csv / .png - oof_predictions.csv - selected_hyperparameters.csv, inner_tuning_scores.csv - importance.csv / .png - confusion_matrices.png - seasonal_profiles.png, gap_filling.csv (baseline only) - final_model_*.joblib - run_config.json, run.log

    Run names: - Baseline: s1s2-<variables>_agyear_tiles-<inner|all>[-<orbit>], e.g. s1s2-s2_agyear_tiles-inner (Sentinel-2 only, no orbit) or s1s2-all_agyear_tiles-inner-asc. - GeoFMs: <model>_agyear_tiles[_2023-24]_<concat|mean>, e.g. clay_agyear_tiles_concat (Morocco), clay_agyear_tiles_2023-24_concat (Utah). - Label-efficiency runs end in _f<percent>_d<draw>, e.g. clay_agyear_tiles_concat_f010_d1.

    6.6 Regions and label efficiency (scripts 03, 03b, 03c)¶

    Setting Meaning Set by
    REGION, VIEWS_NAME study area and its views folder (Morocco views, Utah views_2023-24); folds, features and embeddings are read from outputs/<REGION>/ EXPERIMENT_REGION (run_experiment.py, Slurm)
    TRAIN_FRACTION, FRACTION_DRAW share of the training blocks of each round (1.0 = all; 0.10 / 0.25 / 0.50) and the draw (1–3) EXPERIMENT_FRACTION, EXPERIMENT_DRAW
    N_SEEDS (03b, 03c) trainings per round; the out-of-fold probability is their mean EXPERIMENT_N_SEEDS (1 in label-efficiency runs)
    QUICK_TEST fast check of the whole chain EXPERIMENT_QUICK_TEST

    The block draw is one shared function (models_run/experiment_utils.py, draw_training_blocks()):

    1. Take the training blocks of the round, counting only parcels of the views' agricultural years (Utah's fold file also holds 2021).
    2. Split them into blocks with rainfed parcels and irrigated-only blocks and put each list in a random order (seed from region, round and draw only).
    3. A fraction takes the first blocks of each list (the same share of each, at least one block each). The draws are therefore nested: the 10% blocks are part of the 25% blocks, which are part of the 50% blocks, so a learning curve shows the effect of adding blocks, not of drawing different ones.
    4. The ordering is accepted only if its first 10% already hold at least 20 rainfed parcels (otherwise the next sub-seed); every fraction then keeps enough rainfed parcels (checked: at least 22 in Morocco, 26 in Utah, over all rounds and draws).
    5. Draw k selects the same blocks in scripts 03, 03b and 03c and for every input: the learning curves of all experiments are paired. The validation and test folds stay complete.

    The blocks kept in every round are written to run_config.json (block_draws) and the run log; script 04 reports the mean number of training parcels per fraction.

    Quick checks never mix with results. With QUICK_TEST (or QUICK = True in run_experiment.py / submit_slurm.py) the scripts write to experiments/<E>/results_quick/ and no report is written, so a later real run is never skipped as "already done". The runners also remove every EXPERIMENT_* variable left in the shell and set each run's variables explicitly.

    6.7 Script 03c — train_ltae (neural heads: E03, E04)¶

    Same parcels, folds, PASTIS rotation and output files as script 03 (oof_predictions.csv with model = mlp or ltae), so script 04 compares it directly with the classical classifiers.

    HEAD Input Model Experiment
    mlp the E02 input: 12 months concatenated, training-median fill, standardised, PCA 128 (baseline: the 180 standardised features) MLP 128 → 256 → 128 → 2, batch normalisation, ReLU, dropout 0.2 E03
    ltae the 12 months as a sequence, per-channel standardisation; months without a clear pixel masked in the attention L-TAE (Garnot & Landrieu 2020): channels → d_model 256 + sinusoidal encoding of the mid-month day, 16 heads with one learned master query each (d_k 8), output MLP 256-128, linear layer → 2 E04

    Training: AdamW (learning rate 1e-3, weight decay 1e-4), batch 128, class-weighted cross-entropy, up to 100 epochs, early stopping on the validation fold's macro-F1 (patience 15), best epoch kept; 3 seeds per round. AgriFM (one vector per year) is only used with HEAD = "mlp". Extra outputs: metrics_per_seed.csv, training_history.csv and, for L-TAE, attention_by_month.csv (attention per month and class on the test folds).

    6.8 Script 03b — train_psetae (E05)¶

    PSE-TAE (Garnot et al. 2020) trained from scratch on the pixel sets (view B): 10 Sentinel-2 bands × 12 months, 64 pixels sampled per parcel, the same PASTIS rotation and early stopping. Each run is one config file in experiments/E05_psetae_scratch/configs/ (morocco_s2, utah_s2; morocco_s2s1 is the Sentinel-1 ablation). N_SEEDS = 3 trainings per round, averaged; metrics_per_seed.csv holds the spread. Label efficiency as in §6.6.

    7. Script 04 — compare_models¶

    Purpose: answer the main research question by comparing every input with the baseline, on the same parcels.

    flowchart TD A["oof_predictions.csv<br/>of each run in RUNS"] --> B["Keep parcels predicted in ALL runs"] B --> C["One shared set of 1,000<br/>block-bootstrap samples"] C --> D["Per run × classifier:<br/>macro-F1, rainfed F1,<br/>balanced and overall accuracy, 95% CI"] C --> E["Per GeoFM run × classifier:<br/>Δ macro-F1 vs baseline (same classifier)<br/>95% CI + bootstrap p-value"] E --> F["Holm adjustment over<br/>all comparisons"] D --> OUT["metrics_all_runs.csv<br/>comparison_macro_f1.png"] F --> OUT2["differences_vs_baseline.csv<br/>differs = CI excludes 0 AND Holm p < 0.05"] C --> LC["Label-efficiency runs:<br/>macro-F1 per training fraction<br/>(mean over draws, same samples)"] LC --> OUT3["learning_curve.csv / .png"]
    • Paired: the same bootstrap blocks are used for every run, so each difference is measured on identical resamples (Foody 2009).
    • Multiple comparisons: the experiment's own input × head comparisons with its baseline form one Holm family (runs borrowed from other experiments are not part of it).
    • Same input, earlier experiments' heads (differences_vs_earlier_experiments.csv, own Holm family): every run of this experiment against the runs of the same input in EXTRA_RUNS, e.g. E03 Clay + MLP vs E02 Clay + LogReg / RF / SVM, E04 Clay + L-TAE vs E03 Clay + MLP. This answers Q2 (head) and Q3 (temporal aggregation) with paired tests.
    • Interpretation: an input is better than the baseline only if the 95% interval of Δ macro-F1 lies entirely above 0 and the Holm-adjusted p-value is below 0.05 (column differs; ci_excludes_0 keeps the interval test alone). Before 13 Nov 2026 differs used the interval only; the report pages always applied both conditions.
    • One region at a time (REGION): results are read from experiments/<E>/results/<REGION>/ and written to its comparison/ folder.
    • Runs of other experiments: EXTRA_RUNS lists "<experiment>/<run>" or "<experiment>/*" (all main runs of that experiment in the same region), e.g. E04 compares with "E03_frozen_pca_mlp/*" and "E02_frozen_pca/*". That experiment must have finished first.
    • Baseline: the experiment's own Sentinel-2 baseline run (E03: the baseline with the MLP head; E04: with L-TAE); E05 has none and uses the E02 baseline.
    • Label efficiency: for every run and head, macro-F1 and rainfed F1 at each training fraction, the mean over the draws with a block-bootstrap CI on the same parcels and samples, and the mean training parcels per round (learning_curve.csv, learning_curve.png). Neural label-efficiency runs use one seed per draw; their 100% point is the mean over the main run's 3 seeds scored one by one (oof_per_seed.csv), so every point is single-seed.
    • Missing runs fail loudly: a borrowed experiment without finished runs, or no baseline, stops the script.

    In development mode (EXPERIMENT = None) BASELINE_RUN is the Sentinel-2 baseline s1s2-s2_agyear_tiles-inner; for a run with Sentinel-1 it must match the orbit that was actually used (views_info.json), for example s1s2-all_agyear_tiles-inner-asc.

    7.1 Script 05 — breakdown_oof (results by region, season and purity)¶

    Purpose: test whether the pooled metrics reflect the irrigation signal or the region. Rainfed parcels are concentrated in Doukkala (94%), and Gharb, Tadla and El Haouz contain almost only irrigated parcels, so a classifier can score well by recognising the region (shortcut learning). The 16-px chips also mix each parcel with its neighbours (median purity 0.30). The script reads the out-of-fold predictions of one experiment; no model is trained. It runs per region (REGION) on every head (LogReg, RF, SVM, MLP, L-TAE, PSE-TAE): the focus zone is Doukkala in Morocco and, in Utah, the zone with most rainfed parcels (found automatically; the Bear River basin holds 58% of them); irrigated-only zones are listed for Morocco and found automatically for Utah (≥ 100 parcels, < 2% rainfed).

    Output (experiments/<E>/results/<REGION>/breakdown/) Content
    focus_zone.csv macro-F1 in the focus zone (Morocco: Doukkala, the only zone with both classes) with 95% block-bootstrap CI and the paired difference to the baseline (E01: doukkala.csv)
    irrigated_only_zones.csv share of irrigated parcels predicted rainfed in the irrigated-only zones (Morocco: Gharb, Tadla, El Haouz), with CI
    by_zone_season.csv rainfed recall and precision, false-rainfed rate and macro-F1 per zone and agricultural year
    by_purity.csv, by_area.csv macro-F1 per 16-px patch-purity bin and per parcel-area bin
    breakdown.png focus-zone macro-F1 and false-rainfed rate per input

    First application (E01, nested CV, 8,775 parcels; AgriFM excluded): in Doukkala, no frozen GeoFM exceeds the baseline (baseline SVM 0.896 [0.818–0.952]; the GeoFM random forests are 0.03–0.07 lower, with intervals that exclude 0). In the irrigated-only zones every input labels at most 2% of the irrigated parcels as rainfed, so these zones contribute little to the differences between inputs. The GeoFM advantage in the hardest fold comes from Souss (69 rainfed parcels): rainfed recall 0.75–0.84 for Clay and Prithvi (SVM) against 0.26 for the baseline. GeoFM macro-F1 increases with patch purity more steeply than that of the baseline. The analysis is repeated on E02.

    10. Choices to report and open points¶

    Choice Value Reason
    Frozen encoders, no fine-tuning all four GeoFMs tests the pretrained representation; equal cost for all
    Parcel pooling patch-share-weighted mean describes the parcel, not the surroundings
    AgriFM patch size 4 × 4 px (pretrained), stride 32 px every pretrained weight is used; coarsest grid of the four models (D12)
    Chips 64 × 64 px, patch-aligned centroid at a patch centre (see dataset §7.3)
    Time 12 monthly composites, Sep–Aug covers the winter rainfed cycle and summer irrigation
    Embedding reduction concat + PCA 128, fitted on training folds keeps the timing information; same dimension for every model
    Inputs Sentinel-2 only for every model and the baseline same optical data for all; S1 coverage of Utah changed between years (D14)
    Baseline features inner-pixel medians of 12 S2 bands, NDVI, NDWI, EVI same pixels as the chips
    Validation 5 spatial folds (20 km blocks), per round 3 train / 1 validation / 1 test, as PASTIS estimates mapping of unseen areas; identical for classifiers and neural networks
    Uncertainty block bootstrap, Holm blocks are the independent units

    Open points: - Prithvi on 10 m instead of 30 m data. - AgriFM on 12 monthly composites instead of its dense (5-day) pretraining series (§5.2). - Results per zone, since rainfed parcels are concentrated in Doukkala.
