    Evaluation protocol¶

    This protocol defines the rules common to all pixel-wise experiments (E01–E05) and makes their results comparable across inputs, models and experiments. Any deviation is stated on the experiment's page. The protocol of the earlier parcel-wise benchmark is kept in the archive.

    Contents: 1 Task · 2 Splits · 3 Models · 4 Fairness rules · 5 Metrics · 6 Reading a result · 7 Reporting · References

    1. Task and unit¶

    Every labelled 10 m pixel of the surveyed parcels is classified into one of two classes: 1 irrigated or 2 rainfed (D16, D19). All surveyed parcels are cropland, so the experiments measure the irrigated / rainfed decision alone; no cropland mask is used, and the handling of land that is not cropland is left to the mapping phase (Phase 5). The labelled pixels are the inner pixels of the surveyed parcels, at most 100 per parcel (pixel samples). Every model is scored on the same labelled pixels.

    All experiments run in Morocco first: 2023/24 and 2024/25 pooled, each pixel with its own year (473,675 pixels of 8,775 parcels, 7,579 irrigated and 1,196 rainfed, in 2,015 tiles). Utah (2023/24) follows later with the same plan and is analysed separately.

    2. Spatial splits¶

    Parcels are grouped into 20 km blocks, and the blocks into 5 folds with a similar class balance; a block is never split, and every labelled pixel takes the fold of its parcel's block. Fold assignment: Morocco dataset §5.

    PASTIS rotation (Garnot & Landrieu 2021). Five rounds; in each, 3 folds train, the next fold is the validation fold, one fold is the test fold:

    Round Train Validation (early stopping, settings) Test
    1 2, 3, 4 1 0
    2 3, 4, 0 2 1
    3 4, 0, 1 3 2
    4 0, 1, 2 4 3
    5 1, 2, 3 0 4

    The test fold is never used for any choice. After 5 rounds every labelled pixel has one out-of-fold prediction from a model that never saw its block. Models that work on whole 128 px tiles (E03–E05) predict the test tiles and are read at their labelled pixels; a tile with pixels of several folds contributes only its training-fold labels to the loss.

    Why a rotation rather than one fixed split. (i) Every pixel is tested once, so the rainfed scores rest on all rainfed parcels instead of those of a single test fold. (ii) Five test areas show how strongly accuracy depends on the region. (iii) Settings are never chosen on the test fold, so the reported accuracy is not inflated by tuning (Varma & Simon 2006). Spatially separated folds follow Roberts et al. (2017) and Ploton et al. (2020): random splits of autocorrelated pixels overstate accuracy (Kattenborn et al. 2022).

    3. Models and inputs¶

    All inputs come from the same tile store: 12 monthly Sentinel-2 composites of the agricultural year, Sentinel-2 only (D14).

    Experiment Input Models
    E01 pixel time series, 12 months × 12 bands (+ NDVI, NDWI) random forest, histogram gradient boosting
    E02 same pixel time series TempCNN (the pixel L-TAE is on hold)
    E03 128 × 128 px tiles U-TAE from scratch
    E04 128 × 128 px tiles six frozen foundation models (Prithvi, Clay, TerraMind, AgriFM, Presto, TESSERA) + L-TAE + feature pyramid + UPerNet (Foundation models)
    E05 128 × 128 px tiles, 10, 25, 50 or 100% of the training parcels Prithvi frozen, LoRA, fully fine-tuned, from scratch (four strategies)

    Neural models: class-weighted cross-entropy (weights from the training pixels), early stopping on the validation fold, 3 seeds (42, 1042, 2042) per round; the out-of-fold prediction is the mean probability of the seeds. Settings per experiment are on its page; the shared settings are on the experiment plan.

    4. Fairness rules¶

    flowchart LR subgraph SAME["Identical for every model"] P1["same labelled pixels"] P2["same tile store<br/>(composites, cloud mask, months)"] P3["same folds<br/>and rotation"] P4["same metrics and<br/>bootstrap draws"] end SAME --> D["only the model<br/>(or the one setting the<br/>experiment varies) differs"] D --> C["paired difference<br/>with 95% CI"]
    1. Every fitted step (normalisation statistics, class weights, early stopping) uses the training folds (early stopping: the validation fold); the test fold is used once, for the prediction.
    2. All inputs come from the same tile store.
    3. Models are compared on the same out-of-fold pixels with the same bootstrap draws.
    4. An experiment varies one thing against the experiment it builds on and states it.

    5. Metrics and uncertainty¶

    Metric Role
    Macro-F1 headline: mean F1 of irrigated and rainfed over the labelled pixels
    Rainfed F1, precision, recall the minority class: how many rainfed pixels are found (recall) and how many pixels predicted rainfed are rainfed (precision); the same three scores are kept for irrigated
    Overall accuracy for comparison with the literature only (dominated by the irrigated majority)
    Confusion matrix which way the errors go (rainfed → irrigated or irrigated → rainfed)

    The kappa coefficient is not reported (Pontius & Millones 2011). Results are also given per region, per year (Morocco) and per zone.

    95% confidence intervals: block bootstrap (Künsch 1989). The 20 km blocks, not the pixels, are the independent units. Each of 1,000 draws resamples whole blocks with replacement and recomputes the metric from the summed confusion matrices; the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles give the interval. The same draws are used for every run, so a difference between two models is measured on identical resamples (paired; Foody 2009).

    6. How a result is read¶

    • Δ = model − reference on the same pixels (references: E01 random forest and E03 U-TAE for E01–E04; within E05 the strategies against each other).
    • A model is better (or worse) only if the 95% CI of Δ excludes 0 and the Holm-adjusted p-value is below 0.05 (Holm 1979; correction over all comparisons of a table and metric).
    • A CI that excludes 0 without surviving Holm is reported as "higher / lower, not significant after Holm".
    • Otherwise: no clear difference. With few large blocks per fold, wide CIs are expected.
    • The paired tests are run for the headline macro-F1 and for the rainfed F1.

    7. Reporting standard¶

    Every experiment has one page (docs/experiments/E0<N>_<name>.md) with the question, what changes from the previous experiment, the set-up, how to run it, and, once run, the results with confidence intervals, figures, the paired tests and an interpretation. The comparison table and paired tests of E01–E04 come from pixelwise_run/05_compare_experiments.py; E05 has its own report.py. Finished results are summarised on the Results page.
